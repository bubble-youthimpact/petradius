Pet Behavior & Communication Guide

Understanding your pet’s body language is essential for building trust, preventing anxiety and handling common challenges.

GuidesProductsWebsites & Videos
All guides
View original

1Body language quick reference

Choose a species, then match what you see to how your pet is feeling.

Relaxed & happy

Body posture & tail
Loose body, soft posture, gently wagging tail at neutral height.
Face & eyes
Soft, relaxed eyes; open mouth with relaxed tongue.

Alert & curious

Body posture & tail
Weight forward, ears perked up, tail held horizontally or slightly high.
Face & eyes
Direct gaze, closed mouth or slight focus, intent expression.

Anxious & stressed

Body posture & tail
Tense posture, tail tucked low or wagging stiffly, lip licking, yawning.
Face & eyes
Whites of the eyes showing (“whale eye”), avoiding direct contact.

Fearful / defensive

Body posture & tail
Crouched low, weight shifted back, tail tightly tucked, raised hackles.
Face & eyes
Ears pinned flat, lip curling, growling.

Relaxed & content

Tail & ears
Tail held high with a slight curve at the tip; ears in neutral position.
Body & voice
Stretched out or loosely curled; gentle purring or kneading.

Overstimulated / annoyed

Tail & ears
Tail twitching, flicking or thumping; ears turned outward or back.
Body & voice
Rippling skin on the back, sudden freeze, low growl or vocal protest.

Frightened / threatened

Tail & ears
Puffed-out “bottle-brush” tail; ears flattened completely.
Body & voice
Arched back with fur raised, hissing or spitting.

2Common behavioral challenges

Excessive barking (dogs)

Identify the trigger — boredom, alertness or separation anxiety. Increase exercise and mental stimulation (puzzle toys). Reward quiet behavior immediately with high-value treats rather than yelling.

Destructive scratching (cats)

Scratching is natural marking and stretching. Provide vertical and horizontal sisal scratchers near favorite resting spots. Synthetic pheromone sprays (e.g. Feliway) can reduce stress-related scratching.

Resource guarding (food & toys)

Never forcibly take guarded items away. Practice trade-up games: offer a higher-value treat from a safe distance whenever you approach, to build positive associations.

3Core principles of positive reinforcement

1

Reward promptly

Mark the right behavior within 1–2 seconds with praise or a treat.

2

Ignore or redirect

Avoid physical punishment or harsh reprimands — they increase anxiety and escalation.

3

Keep sessions short

5 to 10 minutes of consistent daily training beats long sessions.

4

Ask a professional

For severe aggression, fear or sudden behavior changes, see a veterinary behaviorist or certified trainer.
